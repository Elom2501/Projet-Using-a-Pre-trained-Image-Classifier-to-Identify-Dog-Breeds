Questions regarding Uploaded Image Classification:

Answer: Yes. All three model architectures classified Dog_01.jpg as a Golden Retriever.
VGG: Golden Retriever

AlexNet: Golden Retriever


2. Did each of the three model architectures classify the breed of dog in Dog_01.jpg to be the same breed of dog as that model architecture classified Dog_02.jpg? If not, report the differences in the classifications.

Answer: No. Each model architecture classified the two uploaded dog images as different breeds.

- VGG: Dog_01.jpg --> Golden Retriever; Dog_02.jpg --> Border Collie
- ResNet: Dog_01.jpg --> Golden Retriever; Dog_02.jpg --> Border Collie
- AlexNet: Dog_01.jpg --> Golden Retriever; Dog_02.jpg --> Border Collie

Therefore, all three architectures agreed on the classifications, but the two dogs were classified as different breeds.


3. Did the three model architectures correctly classify Animal_Name_01.jpg and Object_Name_01.jpg to not be dogs? If not, report the misclassifications.

Answer: Yes. All three model architectures correctly classified both non-dog images as not dogs.

- cat_02.jpg: correctly classified as a cat by VGG, ResNet, and AlexNet.
- Coffee_mug_01.jpg: correctly classified as a cup by VGG, ResNet, and AlexNet.

4. Based upon your answers for questions 1. - 3. above, select the model architecture that you feel did the best at classifying the four uploaded images. Describe why you selected that model architecture as the best on uploaded image classification.

Answer: I selected VGG as the best model architecture. All three architectures correctly classified the four uploaded images as dogs or non-dogs, and all three agreed on the breeds of the two dog images.

However, VGG achieved the best overall performance on the 40-image dataset:

- VGG: 87.5% overall match, 100% correct dog classification, 100% correct non-dog classification, and 93.33% correct breed classification.
- ResNet: 82.5% overall match, 100% correct dog classification, 90% correct non-dog classification, and 90% correct breed classification.
- AlexNet: 75.0% overall match, 100% correct dog classification, 100% correct non-dog classification, and 80% correct breed classification.

Therefore, VGG was the best-performing architecture overall, particularly because it achieved the highest breed classification accuracy and the highest overall percentage of matching classifications.
